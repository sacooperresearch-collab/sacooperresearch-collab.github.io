Publications

From Scratch: A Direct Test of the Sound-Horizon Assumption
S. A. Cooper · Preprint (2025)

An observational evaluation of the sound-horizon assumption using late-time expansion measurements and the directly measured CMB acoustic angle. The analysis shows that, without invoking early-universe physics, current data do not empirically determine the sound horizon with precision sufficient for use as an absolute calibration scale.

From Scratch II: An Empirical Test of the Flat-Universe Assumption
S. A. Cooper · Preprint (2025)

This paper evaluates the spatial curvature parameter using only late-time observations, without assuming a sound horizon, recombination physics, or CMB-based priors. The results show that late-time data constrain curvature only weakly, leaving spatial geometry empirically underdetermined.

From Scratch III: The Structural Incoherence of Last Scattering
S. A. Cooper · Preprint (2025)

This work examines the role of last scattering as a calibration anchor in cosmology. It shows that the physical recombination process lacks the structural properties required to fix absolute distance scales, explaining why parameter shifts occur without convergence across datasets.

From Scratch IV: The Cosmic Microwave Background Is Not a Record of Early Time
S. A. Cooper · Preprint (2026)

This paper tests whether large-angle CMB temperature anisotropies behave as a preserved record of early cosmic conditions. Conditioning experiments show that the observed signal responds coherently to present-day sky structure, falsifying the interpretation of the CMB as an observationally autonomous primordial imprint.
